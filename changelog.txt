Merlin Browser changelog
========================

Newest first. Each entry starts with its version on its own line, which is how
the updater finds the notes for a release. The version number itself lives in
version.txt and nowhere else.

1.8.18
  A page that checks for robots, met in a Merlin Engine tab, opens with Chromium
  in that tab's place, at the page that was asked for. Google answers Merlin
  Engine's searches with its "unusual traffic" page every time: it tells
  programs from browsers by how their connections are made, and Merlin Engine's
  are Python's; its puzzle is in a frame Merlin Engine does not draw. Chromium
  is a browser to it, and shows the results. Cloudflare's "Just a moment..." is
  met the same way. The status bar says why.
  window.postMessage, a message event to the window, as in a browser:
  reCAPTCHA's script had stopped on it.
  Buttons sized to their content. Measured, a width that is a share of the room
  (100%) counts as auto, as CSS has it: AlterniTech's two buttons, each 100% of
  its wrapper, had measured 50,000 pixels wide. A box's padding and border
  count where it ends, painted or not: a button with no background had measured
  without its right-hand padding. And a line holds text given exactly its own
  width, to a pixel: words measured one by one come to a hair more than the
  phrase measured whole, and "Free Software" and "All Products" had wrapped.
  Not yet: Hugging Face's layout; Google's AI Mode button (the Google page in
  the zip had none to look at).

1.8.17
  Quirks mode. A page with no doctype (or an old one browsers treat so) is
  drawn by browsers' quirks rules, as the HTML specification gives them: a
  table starts its text afresh, inheriting no text-align, white-space,
  line-height or font from outside it. Hacker News has no doctype and is laid
  out in a table inside <center>: its every story had been centred, where in a
  browser they are left-aligned. So with many older pages.
  The page's mode is kept through its scripts: it goes to them without a
  doctype if it came without one, comes back so, and document.compatMode says
  BackCompat. Every page had gone to its scripts with <!DOCTYPE html>.
  <center>, and align="center" on a table or its cell, centre the boxes in them,
  not their text only, as browsers' -webkit-center does: Hacker News' 85% table
  sits in the middle of the page.

1.8.16
  Background pictures. background-image: url(), and url() in the background
  shorthand, had never been drawn, only gradients: every photo set as a
  section's backdrop on the web was missing, and AlterniTech's hero, a photo
  under a dark overlay, was the overlay alone. Picture layers are kept with the
  gradients, in order; a stylesheet's url()s are made whole against its own
  address; the pictures are fetched as the page's images are; and each is drawn
  by background-size (cover, contain, auto, lengths and percentages),
  background-position (keywords, percentages, lengths) and background-repeat
  (repeat by default, no-repeat, repeat-x, repeat-y), clipped to its box and
  rounded corners. The shorthand gives each layer its size, position and repeat.
  A block inside an inline element is laid out as a block. Browsers split the
  inline round it; here the inline is laid out as the block that holds it.
  GitHub's header is a block inside a <react-partial>, inline as any unknown
  element is: it had no box, its black bar was missing and its parts spread
  down a thousand pixels of white while the page was new. So with any card
  wrapped in an <a>.
  A flex item in a column is never smaller than its content, unless it clips
  what overflows: its automatic minimum size, as CSS has it. Square's column
  held AlterniTech's products at height: 100%, laid out 355 high: the footer
  came after the first row of products, the rest drawn beneath it.

1.8.15
  Images laid out as blocks are drawn. An <img> with display: block, or a flex
  or grid item, had been given its box and never its picture: Tailwind makes
  every image display: block, and galleries, cards and heroes are flex and grid,
  so on a great many sites most pictures were missing. Such an image is as wide
  as its picture unless told otherwise, and keeps its proportions to its
  content's width, padding and border apart.
  object-fit (contain, cover, none, scale-down) and object-position: a picture
  in a box of another shape had always been stretched to it, sites' photos
  warped and enlarged. cover fills its box and is cropped, contain fits whole,
  placed as object-position says, centred by default.
  Merlin's new-tab tiles fit their names again. A flex item's size, worked out
  without its padding and border, had them taken off once more under 1.8.14's
  border-box: the tiles came out narrower than their names, the search box too
  short for its text.
  Not yet: CSS background pictures (background-image: url(), and url() in the
  background shorthand) are not drawn at all, only gradients: AlterniTech's hero
  photo, under its dark overlay, is the overlay alone. Next.

1.8.14
  box-sizing: border-box for widths. A width, min-width or max-width given for
  the border box holds its padding and border, as heights now do too: a 200px
  box of border-box had come out 250 wide. Nearly every site sets border-box on
  everything, so anything given a width on them had been too wide by its
  padding and border. Heights (height, min-height, max-height) likewise:
  Google's 40px Sign in button had grown to 60.
  Natural widths, as CSS has them. An absolutely placed box takes no part in
  its container's size, and an auto-width block is as wide as what it holds:
  measured, each had counted the whole room it was measured in. Square's menu
  items each held a drop-down placed absolutely and an empty bordered
  underline, measured 100,000 pixels wide, and stacked one a line; they now sit
  in a row, as on any site whose menu items hold drop-downs.
  ResizeObserver reports elements' real sizes, from Merlin Engine's layout,
  and again whenever a layout changes them. It had always said 0 high: Square
  sizes AlterniTech's logo from it, and drew it 0 pixels tall.
  The old prefixed display values (-webkit-box, -webkit-inline-box,
  -webkit-flex, -ms-flexbox, -ms-grid), which sites still use, -webkit-box with
  -webkit-line-clamp for clamped text: they had made no box at all.
  Not yet: Google's "+" in the search box is still drawn large, and the box sits
  a little left of centre; an inline-block with a min-height still stands a
  little tall on its line; GitHub's blank top while it loads.

1.8.13
  Merlin opens when asked. A new launch hands its links to a Merlin already
  running only if that Merlin answers that it took them. One still ending (its
  window gone, its event loop stopped) could still be connected to, and every
  launch had handed over to it and quit: the log showed launch after launch
  doing so for minutes after a clean exit, Merlin seeming slow to open until
  that process was gone. Now one that does not answer within two seconds is
  passed by, and a new Merlin starts.
  Start-up's stages are written to merlin-log.txt with their times (Qt loaded,
  window made, window shown...), so a slow start shows where its time goes.
  Hugging Face's app runs. Node.prototype's own getters (firstChild,
  childNodes, textContent...) were the DOM library's generic ones, null for
  every node, the real ones being its subclasses'. Svelte takes firstChild's
  getter from Node.prototype and calls it on every node, as is right in a
  browser, and got null, so Hugging Face's app stopped as it began. Each now
  hands on to the node's own class. Replayed from its zip, Hugging Face's code
  runs without an error.
  An Error a page logs itself (its own error catcher, as Hugging Face's) comes
  to the console with where it was thrown and the code there.
  From the zips: AlterniTech's site and Google's page run without an error.

1.8.12
  A page's comments reach its scripts, each in its place. Svelte, Vue and React
  mark where their components are with comments (<!--[-->, <!--]-->) and
  hydrate from those marks; Merlin Engine's parser had dropped them, and Hugging
  Face's app found nothing to start from, its page never brought to life. A
  comment also keeps the text on each side of it apart, as Svelte's <!---->
  between two texts means it to. They are not drawn.
  An image a page's script waits on fires load or error as it really went, with
  its natural size: a page that tries a picture directly and falls back on
  error (Ponder, to its own image proxy, for thumbnails a site will not serve
  to others) had been told every one loaded, and its image tiles stayed empty.
  new Image() to preload is fetched as the page's images are.
  Web fonts are fetched in the form that reads everywhere first: WOFF, then TTF
  and OTF, then WOFF2. Qt on Windows cannot read WOFF2, and taking each font's
  first source alone (WOFF2, as Tabler's icon font gives it) had left every
  icon on Ponder a box.
  localhost is reached on IPv4 first. Windows gives ::1 first, and a server
  listening on 127.0.0.1 alone, as Python's do, Ponder's among them, was
  reached only after that failed: two seconds a request.
  The root element's client width and height are the viewport's, as browsers
  give them. Square reads documentElement.clientWidth to choose its layout and
  had drawn AlterniTech's site for a phone: its menu stacked, its buttons
  cramped. Replayed from its zip, it now chooses the desktop layout.
  Form fields' validation (setCustomValidity, checkValidity, reportValidity,
  validity, validationMessage), which Square's fields use.
  The debugging zip is written under a passing name and given its own only
  once whole: Hugging Face's, with over a thousand modules, had looked finished
  in Downloads while still being written, and was sent cut short.

1.8.11
  A stylesheet or preload <link> a page's script adds, and an image given a
  src (new Image() too), fire their load, as in a browser. Webpack loads a
  route's CSS with a <link> and waits for its load: none came, and Square's
  router waited for ever on its first page. AlterniTech's site, its data all
  there, was never shown: replayed from its debugging zip, the router now
  finishes its first navigation where it had stayed pending, and the page's
  components are made (123 where there had been 59).
  A whole TreeWalker, as the DOM specification gives it (parentNode,
  firstChild, lastChild, previousSibling, nextSibling, previousNode, nextNode,
  with filters and whatToShow), and NodeFilter's constants, which were missing:
  a walker for elements had walked text too. GitHub's and YouTube's code use
  them.
  performance.timing and performance.navigation, which YouTube reads as it
  starts, and document.implementation.createHTMLDocument, which its web
  components library uses.
  From the zips: AlterniTech's network log showed every request answered, so
  the page's data was not what was missing; Ponder's showed none failing and
  no errors. GitHub's repository page, with 1.8.10's fixes, now has its Code
  button, 1 Branch and 7 Tags, the latest commit's line laid out with its 123
  commits, and the Contributors.
  Not yet: the column headings over GitHub's file list, which GitHub hides,
  show; its branch name loses a letter ("mai"). YouTube plays no video.

1.8.10
  Escaped class names match, as CSS reads them. Tailwind writes its classes
  with backslashes (.w-1\/2, .lg\:flex, .size-\[\.6rem\], .\32 xl\:p-4) and
  none of them had matched: on every Tailwind site most of the styling was
  missing, Hugging Face's huge icons and wrong layout among it. Names may hold
  any character beyond ASCII too, as Square's class names hold emoji. The one
  space after a hex escape is part of it, not a descendant combinator, and an
  escaped bracket is no bracket.
  A grid's natural width is what its content needs. Measured, it had been laid
  out in 100,000 pixels and centred there: GitHub's Code button's content came
  to 50,000 pixels wide, and its label was drawn far off to the right, leaving
  the button blank. The branch button's name, squeezed beside it, had lost a
  letter ("mai").
  A page's own button for a file input (input.click() or showPicker(), as
  Ponder's Browse for a wallpaper) opens Merlin's file dialog, by the input's
  accept and multiple; what is chosen reaches the page as Files in
  input.files, with input and change, read as in a browser (FileReader).
  Files chosen with Merlin's own file field reach the page's scripts too.
  Not yet: Google answers Merlin Engine's searches with its "unusual traffic"
  page. It tells browsers from programs by how the connection is made (TLS and
  HTTP/2), not by anything a page sends, and Merlin Engine's connections are
  Python's. Ponder, giving Google's results through Serper, searches instead.

1.8.9
  ::before and ::after in Merlin Engine. Their rules are matched with the
  element's, their style inherits from it, and where content gives them some
  they are boxes at the start and end of it, laid out and drawn like any other:
  icon fonts' icons (Tabler, Font Awesome, Bootstrap Icons: Ponder's settings
  cog among them), quotes, separators, attr(), badges and the clearfix. content
  takes strings with their CSS escapes ("\eb20" being an icon font's
  character), attr(), and open and close quotes; none and normal make no box;
  images and replaced elements have none, as in a browser. Scripts never see
  them, as in a browser.
  IntersectionObserver counts a box with no size as in view where it lies in
  view, as browsers do: a page that fills an empty placeholder once it is seen
  had waited for ever.
  The debugging zip carries a network log (network.txt): every request a page's
  scripts made, with its method, status, size and time. A page left empty with
  nothing in its console, as a Square site can be, shows there which data
  request failed.
  From the 1.8.8 zips: GitHub's repository page now shows each file's last
  commit and date, and its releases, its code view's scripts running now that
  window events reach them.

1.8.8
  Secure DNS, as Brave has it: site names are looked up over HTTPS
  (Cloudflare, then Google, reached by address), not with Windows' or the
  router's own DNS, which can hold an old address after a site's records change
  and can be read on the way. Certificates are still checked against the site's
  own name. Local names (localhost, addresses, names with no dot, .local) use
  the system, and so does everything if neither service can be reached.
  Chromium tabs are given it too, secure with a fallback. Settings > Merlin
  Engine > "Use secure DNS (DNS over HTTPS)", on by default.
  Enter in a search box: to the page's scripts first, as a keydown, as in a
  browser, and the form is sent only if they do not take it; in a textarea, a
  newline only if they do not. form.submit() and requestSubmit() send the form
  through Merlin; they had done nothing. Google's search box (a textarea whose
  keydown sends the search) and Ponder's search now search on Enter.
  The window's own events reach pages: load (once, after the page's scripts),
  scroll, resize and popstate. The window is Deno's event target, which took
  only Deno's events, and every one from the page's DOM had failed on it,
  quietly: no page had ever heard its window load or scroll. GitHub's code view
  and landing page scripts stopped on it. PopStateEvent, HashChangeEvent,
  SubmitEvent and the other event classes missing, carrying what they are made
  with.
  An error in a timer, through Node's timers as scripts in node:vm use them,
  had ended the whole process: it is reported, and the page goes on.
  The status bar no longer stays at "Loading... 60%": a page is loaded once it
  is shown, laid out, rather than after a layout with none waiting behind it,
  which never came on a page whose scripts change it all the time.
  The debugging zip, and Save page as > zip, are written in the background:
  with a page's modules (Hugging Face's are over a thousand), writing them had
  frozen the browser.
  The new-tab button's plus is drawn through its very centre: a "+" of text sat
  where Windows' font put it, off centre.

1.8.7
  The start page's Wikipedia tile is now Convert Files (http://convert.to.it/),
  in a new install and, once, in an existing one where the Wikipedia tile is
  still there; a tile removed or added after stays so.
  GitHub's profile and repository pages lay out as on GitHub. Four layout
  fixes, none of them GitHub's alone:
  - A grid track may be a calc() of a percentage and lengths, as GitHub sizes
    its main column: calc(100% - sidebar - gutter). Read as auto, it shrank to
    a sliver, a word to a line.
  - Room left in a grid first grows tracks with a definite most towards it,
    before auto tracks are stretched, as the specification orders it.
  - The clearfix (.x::after { clear: both }) contains its floats. GitHub's
    floated profile name had run on and put the sidebar's details beside it.
  - Flex items honour box-sizing: border-box, their width taking in padding
    and border. Two at 50% with padding had wrapped to one a row.
  Google: its search box takes typing. It is a textarea given display: flex,
  which had been laid out as a flex box with no field in it; a form field is
  now a field whatever its display. document.styleSheets, with insertRule,
  which Google's CSS loader uses.
  Hugging Face no longer slows the whole browser. An infinite animation of no
  duration made every frame fail, about thirty times a second: such an
  animation is now over at once, as CSS has it; an animation that cannot be
  drawn is drawn still and not tried again; and a duration given by var(), as
  animate.css gives it, is read. And a page whose scripts change it all the
  time is shown at a pace the browser can bear: after each update the next
  waits at least 0.3 seconds, or twice what the last took, the latest change
  always shown.
  The fallback from https to http now covers what is entered in the address
  bar whether typed or completed from history (the completion had put
  https:// in front, and the fallback never came), and a site found to answer
  only on http is remembered for the session, so reloads and links reach it.

1.8.6
  Google shows again in Merlin Engine. Its page holds a <noscript> block for
  browsers without scripts, which hides every div and sends them to its basic
  page; a browser running scripts ignores all of it, and Merlin Engine had
  applied it on top of the page Google's scripts built. Where a page's
  scripts run, <noscript> is now nothing, its styles and refreshes included;
  where they do not, it is shown, as before.
  A generic font family (sans-serif, serif, monospace, system-ui) now stands
  for real installed fonts in its place in the list, and emoji fonts come after
  every font for text. Before, a generic name was only a hint, and a font named
  after it drew the text: Google's list ends in Noto Color Emoji, whose wide
  spaces opened gaps between every word.
  The download popup follows the theme: its panel, border and text are the
  light or dark theme's, its buttons are the theme's own, and a change of theme
  is picked up the next time it shows. Its labels had taken the light theme's
  white background under light text.
  JavaScript, from the 1.8.5 debugging zips, which now carry the sites' own
  scripts:
  - Links have their address's parts (protocol, host, hostname, port,
    pathname, search, hash, origin) and a whole href, as in a browser. Square's
    sites take addresses apart with them, and stopped without: AlterniTech's
    site was left a white page.
  - document.getElementsByName, which GitHub's behaviours script uses.
  - A second definition of a custom element is refused with a NotSupportedError,
    as browsers refuse it. GitHub defines some twice and expects that refusal.
  The debugging zip now also carries the page as it came from the server
  (original.html), so with its scripts a page can be run again from the start.

1.8.5
  CSS animations in Merlin Engine. @keyframes are read from every stylesheet,
  inside @media and @supports too, and the animation property and its parts:
  name, duration, timing function (ease and its kin, cubic-bezier(), steps()),
  delay, iteration count, direction, fill mode and play state, several to an
  element. Animations of transform and opacity are played as browsers play
  them, by drawing again, not laying out again: marquees such as GitHub's logo
  bar, spinners, fades and slides. A page is drawn again about 30 times a
  second only where something animated is in view, and not at all otherwise.
  2D and 3D transforms, all of them: translate, scale, rotate and skew in each
  form, rotateX, rotateY and rotate3d, translateZ, matrix(), matrix3d() and
  perspective(); the translate, rotate and scale properties; transform-origin;
  a parent's perspective and perspective-origin, so a card turned with
  rotateY() is drawn foreshortened as in a browser; and backface-visibility.
  calc() of a percentage and pixels in a transform, as GitHub's marquee ends
  at translateX(calc(-100% - gap)). A transformed element is a stacking
  context, as in CSS. Translation alone still moves links with it; links in
  rotated or scaled elements stay where they were laid out, for now.
  The arrows by GitHub's menu names point down, as in Brave: they are turned
  with rotate(), which had been left out.
  A box counts towards the page's height whether or not anything is drawn in
  it: an empty absolutely placed box below the rest had been out of reach.
  Save page as > the debugging zip now carries the scripts the page ran: plain
  scripts as Merlin fetched them, and its modules from Deno's cache. A page
  can then be run again elsewhere, offline, with its own code. Scripts only:
  not the data a page fetches.
  Not yet: CSS transitions (changes eased over time when a page's classes
  change), filter and backdrop-filter (blur), and mask-image.

1.8.4
  An address typed with no scheme is taken as https, as before; if its site
  does not answer on https at all, it is now tried over http, as Brave does,
  and the status bar says it is not secure. alternitech.co.uk answers only on
  http. A link or an address given as https:// is never opened over http.
  A server's own page for an error (a site's 404 page, Google's page for a
  429) is shown, as browsers show it, rather than Merlin Engine's own.
  Modern CSS colours: color-mix() (in srgb, srgb-linear and oklab, with
  premultiplied alpha and the specification's percentages), oklch(), oklab(),
  hwb(), color(), light-dark() and relative colours (rgb(from ...)). GitHub
  uses color-mix() 102 times; unread, its faint lines became the text colour
  and were drawn solid around everything.
  JavaScript, from the debugging zips of GitHub, Google and Hugging Face:
  - Node's globals (process, Buffer, global, setImmediate), which Deno has and
    browsers do not, are gone. GitHub's code saw process, took itself to be in
    Node, and its landing page script, which fades the opening section, failed.
  - The CSS object (CSS.escape, CSS.supports), which GitHub and Google use.
  - Selectors the DOM does not know (:target, :hover, :focus-visible...) match
    nothing rather than throwing; :defined matches all.
  - Plain scripts run as real scripts, so their top-level const, let and class
    are seen by the scripts and modules after them, as in a browser.
  - import() in a plain script or inline module, and an inline module's
    relative imports, are resolved against the page's address. Hugging Face's
    import("/front/build/...") had been resolved against Merlin's own folder.

1.8.3
  The download popup's corners are as round as the window's (Settings >
  Appearance), and its panel is painted by Merlin: on a see-through window Qt
  paints no stylesheet background, which would have left its text floating.
  A connection that fails (refused, reset, timed out, the name not found yet)
  is tried twice more, with "Retrying connection... (2 of 3)" in the status
  bar, before Merlin Engine says the page could not be opened. A site that
  answers on a later try had shown "could not open" first. A server's own
  answer, such as 404, and certificate problems are not retried.
  JavaScript, from the debugging zips of GitHub and a Square site:
  - Scripts run in the order HTML gives them: those read as the page is
    parsed, then defer scripts and modules together in their order, then
    async ones. Square's sites put their main scripts under defer, after the
    inline scripts that set them up; run in plain order, they found nothing
    set up, stopped, and the site stayed a white page.
  - An uncaught error or rejected promise, in a timer, frame or event, is
    reported and the page's scripts go on, as in a browser. Deno ends its
    process on one, and a single error had ended all of GitHub's scripts.
  - document.domain, characterSet, compatMode, contentType, fonts,
    scrollingElement, activeElement and others, which the DOM lacked. GitHub
    stopped on "Unable to get document domain".
  - An error in the console now comes with the page's code where it went
    wrong, marked, so a debugging zip shows what a site tripped on.

1.8.2
  A finished download says so in a popup under the downloads button, as Brave
  does: the file's name, its size and folder, and Open and Show in folder (on
  Windows, Explorer with the file chosen). It takes no focus, so typing in a
  page goes on, and goes after a few seconds, or stays while the pointer is on
  it. For downloads in either engine and for Save page as; a Chromium download
  that is cancelled or fails says so in the status bar instead.
  GitHub's scripts now load in Merlin Engine. What the debugging zip from
  1.8.1 showed, and what is put right:
  - GitHub's modules import names such as react/jsx-runtime, which the page's
    import map (<script type="importmap">) turns into addresses. Deno did not
    know the page's map, and those modules, landing-pages among them, failed.
    The map is now given to Deno, its hosts allowed for imports.
  - GitHub's custom elements assign observedAttributes to their classes, and
    the DOM's base class had it as a getter only, so they failed to load. It is
    assignable now, as in a browser.
  - Scripts were told every element was 0 by 0, at 0,0, and that everything
    was in view. Each layout is now given to them: getBoundingClientRect,
    offsetWidth and the rest give real sizes and places, and
    IntersectionObserver reports elements coming into and going out of view as
    the page scrolls, by its thresholds and rootMargin, as in a browser. A
    section that fades as the page scrolls past it, as GitHub's opening one,
    can now do so. The page's elements are numbered by Merlin before scripts
    start, so even their first measurements are real.
  - The scripts' process had ended on GitHub with nothing said of why. Deno's
    own messages are now kept, and the last of them go in merlin-log.txt and
    the debugging zip when it ends; it is given 2 GB rather than 1.

1.8.1
  JavaScript is on by default in Merlin Engine, as in other browsers, with one
  switch: Settings > Merlin Engine > "Run JavaScript". The per-site permission,
  its main menu entry, its Settings button and the "Allow JavaScript" offer are
  gone. Deno, which runs pages' JavaScript, is fetched once, in the background,
  the first time a page has scripts, and the page is reloaded when it is ready.
  Merlin Engine draws every page by default. Settings saved before this version
  are moved over to it once; after that, Settings > Merlin Engine decides.
  Chromium remains for what Merlin Engine cannot do yet, YouTube and video
  among them: "Open the current page in the other engine" in Settings. The
  old "always open this site in Chromium" list is gone.
  Why a page's scripts did or did not run is now recorded: off in Settings,
  Deno still being fetched, started, running, or ended, with the reason in
  merlin-log.txt when they fail. Save page as > the debugging zip carries it,
  whether the scripts changed the page, and the page's console.
  A module that never finishes loading is no longer waited on for ever: each
  is given 20 seconds, and the page is sent back as the classic scripts left
  it without waiting for the modules. GitHub's scripts are almost all modules,
  and on GitHub its page never came back changed.

1.8.0
  JavaScript in Merlin Engine. It is off until you allow it for a site: when a
  page needs it the notice bar offers "Allow JavaScript", and the main menu's
  "JavaScript on this site" turns it on and off; Settings > Merlin Engine
  forgets the sites allowed.
  Pages' scripts run in V8, Chrome's JavaScript engine, by way of Deno, which
  Merlin already fetches for yt-dlp and fetches when first wanted if it has
  not. Each tab's scripts run in a Deno process of their own, given no
  permissions at all: no files, no network, nothing of the computer. It may
  import modules only from the page's own hosts. Everything else a page asks
  for (fetch, XMLHttpRequest, scripts) Merlin does on its behalf, with the
  page's own cookies and through the content blocker. The process ends when
  the tab leaves the page or closes.
  The page's DOM lives in that process (linkedom, open source, shipped with
  Merlin), with what pages expect of a browser: window, document, location,
  history, navigator, document.cookie (never HttpOnly cookies), localStorage
  (kept for each site, not in private windows) and sessionStorage, timers,

1.7.3.2
  Merlin Engine styles pages in worker processes of their own. Python runs
  one thread at a time, so styling a big page on a background thread, as
  1.7.3.1 did, still took turns with everything else in Merlin and slowed it
  all while GitHub loaded. A worker is a separate process with its own Python,
  running on another processor core alongside Merlin: this is how Chromium
  keeps each tab's work apart too. Merlin's own share of styling a GitHub-sized
  page falls from about a second to a few hundredths. Two workers run, so two
  tabs can load at once; each keeps the sites' parsed stylesheets, so a site
  is quicker the second time. They start when first needed, load no Qt, and
  close with Merlin. A worker that dies is replaced; if none can start, pages
  are styled as before, so a worker can make a page slower, never break it.
  Restyling, when the window is resized past a site's breakpoint, a <details>
  is opened, or shields are switched, happens there too, in the background.
  position: sticky works: a sticky header keeps its place until scrolled to,
  then holds at the top of the window while its section passes, and leaves
  with it, its links and fields with it. It had been left where it first fell,
